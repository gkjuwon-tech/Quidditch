Safety · the whole point

Three rules. Held every tick.

A flying contact sport sounds like a lawsuit. It is — unless the safety is the product, not an afterthought. So it is the product. Every claim below is checked by the test suite on every simulated tick.

Invariant I

Nobody falls.

The rider's enemy isn't the other team — it's gravity. We engineer against gravity.

  • Fly-by-intent. The human asks for a velocity, capped to a safe envelope. They cannot command an unrecoverable bank.
  • Geofenced pitch. Slam the sticks into a wall and a stopping-distance floor brakes you to a halt before it.
  • Hold on release. Centre the controls and the broom parks itself in the air.
  • The kill switch lands you. The big red button is a gentle, synchronised descent — measured ~1.4 m/s — never a motor cut. On a manned craft, that distinction is everything.
Invariant II

Nobody gets hit.

Every ball is a foam shell over a micro-drone. The menace is real; the contact is not.

  • A stopping-distance floor sits under every ball — the gentle Quaffle and the aggressive Bludger alike. It decelerates the shell to a stop at its safety distance from any person, and accounts for the person's motion too.
  • The Bludger "tag" is a proximity event at ~1.5 m, outside the floor — so the shell never has to reach you. A hit is judged in software, not in bone.
  • Measured: across a full match with three balls and four players, the closest any ball came to anyone was +0.48 m of clear air. The invariant held on every tick.
Invariant III

Nothing collides.

A central referee sees everyone and arbitrates what no single craft can.

  • Broom-on-broom deconfliction. Two players diving for the same Snitch close to 0.48 m without the referee — and are held 1.6 m apart with it.
  • Three independent layers. A central predictor, each craft's own avoidance, and the physical foam — fail one, fail two, the person is still fine.
  • One master kill. A single call sends every craft into a controlled descent at once.
“Magic? No — we just wrote the collision-avoidance really well.” — the safety thesis
