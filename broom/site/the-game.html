The Game

A sport with a Z-axis.

Football is 22 people in a plane. Every human sport is stuck in 2D — we left the sky empty. This one fills it: seven a side, three kinds of ball, scoring hoops at three heights, and a small golden thing that does not want to be caught.

The pieces

🧹 The Broom

A one-person eVTOL you ride. You don't pilot it — you express intent and the computer flies. Let go of the controls and it parks itself in mid-air. Lean and it carries you, capped to a safe envelope. You cannot crash it by being bad at flying.

🔴 The Quaffle

The friendly ball. Soft hover, generous to catch; carry it and throw it through a hoop for ten. A tunable aim-assist makes it forgiving for a youth league and ruthless for the pros.

⚫ The Bludger

The menace — minus the cracked skulls. It hunts a player and "tags" them from a metre and a half away as a software hit, then peels off. All threat, zero contact. Swing a bat and it's deflected.

🟡 The Golden Snitch

Tiny, absurdly agile, and built to evade. It predicts a seeker's reaching hand and is already gone. One seeker can't catch it; it takes a coordinated pair. Catch it: +150, and the match ends.

How it's played

  • Seven a side. Chasers, Beaters, a Keeper, a Seeker — all on brooms, all flown by the same stack.
  • Score with the Quaffle through any of three hoops, set at three heights, for 10 points.
  • Survive the Bludgers. Get tagged and a central referee sends you off for a short penalty.
  • End it with the Snitch. +150 and game over — so the chase has stakes the whole match.
  • The invisible rules: the pitch is geofenced, brooms never collide, and no ball ever drives into a person.
Why now

In 2001 it needed magic. In 2026 it needs a LiDAR.

Every part is finally on the shelf. eVTOL has flown people thousands of times. Drone swarms paint the sky at the Olympics. Predictive collision-avoidance already beats human drone-racers. Nobody assembled them into a sport — because the people who could do the engineering hadn't read the books, and the people who'd read the books couldn't do the engineering. We did the hard part first.
