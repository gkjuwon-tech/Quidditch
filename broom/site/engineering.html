The stack, top to bottom

The broom — fly-by-intent

A PX4-style cascade (position → attitude → rate → mixer) over a 6-DOF plant. Sticks centred = hold position, altitude and heading. Slam them at a wall and a geofence holds you in. The kill switch is a gentle descent, never a motor cut.

Estimation — a 15-state EKF

Error-state EKF fusing a noisy IMU, GNSS and a magnetometer. Flying on the estimate alone — no ground truth — it holds attitude to about two degrees through 9 m/s gusting wind, decimated to 100 Hz so a whole match runs faster than real time.

Polyglot — a Rust real-time core

The hot inner loop is ported to a dependency-light Rust core, behaviour-identical to the Python reference to ~1e-13. Worst-case tick: 158 µs vs 2214 µs — 14× tighter, well inside the 2500 µs budget at 400 Hz. It cross-compiles to a real flight MCU: ~13 KB, zero heap.

The match — players + a referee

Players aren't scripted dots; each is a broom flown by the whole stack. A central referee deconflicts brooms, runs the rulebook (tag → send-off, hoop → +10, Snitch → +150), and can drop everyone into a controlled descent with one call.

Latency is a safety property

Why the safety loop is compiled.

At 400 Hz you have 2500 µs per tick. One garbage-collection pause blows the deadline; on a manned craft a blown deadline is a destabilised loop. So the reflexes are Rust.
